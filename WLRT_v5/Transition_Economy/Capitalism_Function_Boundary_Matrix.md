# Capitalism: Function–Boundary Matrix

**Status:** Developing

## Method

Each mechanism is examined through:

Function
→ Benefit
→ Fragility
→ Boundary
→ Replacement

Replacement means preservation of function through transformation
where possible, not automatic elimination of the mechanism.

---

## 1. Price

**Function**  
Information compression and coordination.

**Benefit**  
Allows heterogeneous resources and preferences to be compared through
a common coordinate.

**Fragility**  
Multidimensional system state is compressed into a signal that does
not necessarily represent fragility, reversibility, redistribution
capability, or loss of future trajectories.

**Boundary**  
Price becomes insufficient when decisions that are locally rational
according to price systematically degrade wider system state.

**Potential Replacement / Extension**

Price
→ Price + State Diagnostics
→ State-Constrained Decisions

**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / DEVELOPING

---

## 2. Money

**Function**  
Universal exchange, coordination, access routing, and temporal
transfer.

**Benefit**  
Reduces coordination complexity across heterogeneous agents.

**Fragility**  
Access to real resources becomes strongly dependent on access to the
monetary layer.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

**Potential Replacement / Extension**  
Hybrid monetary and non-monetary access mechanisms.

**Status:** DEVELOPING

---
## 3. Capital

**Function**

Capital performs several related functions:

- accumulation of economic capacity through time;
- concentration of resources;
- financing of large-scale projects;
- absorption of entrepreneurial risk;
- selection among competing future opportunities.

Capital allocation therefore acts partly as a mechanism through which
the economic system selects future trajectories.

**Benefit**

Capital allows dispersed resources to be concentrated and directed
toward activities whose results may occur only in the future.

It supports:

- investment;
- experimentation;
- scaling;
- innovation;
- long-horizon projects.

**Fragility**

Capital accumulation may create positive feedback:

Capital
→ Access
→ Information
→ Opportunity
→ Influence
→ More Capital

As this process develops, capital may become not only a result of
successful redistribution but also a mechanism for influencing future
redistributions.

Accumulation may also increase concentration and reduce the number of
independent redistribution paths available to the wider system.

**Boundary**

Capital accumulation becomes structurally problematic when the loss
of independent future redistribution paths and the increase in
systemic fragility begin to exceed the systemic benefits generated by
additional capital concentration.

A system may therefore experience:

Financial Wealth ↑

while:

Redistribution Capability ↓

**Potential Replacement / Extension**

Capital
→ Capital + State Diagnostics
→ State-Constrained Capital Allocation

Capital remains an allocation mechanism, but its optimization occurs
within an admissible adaptive state space.

**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / DEVELOPING

---## 4. Profit

**Function**

Profit provides decentralized feedback about the economic result of an
activity.

Conceptually:

Capital
→ Activity
→ Profit / Loss
→ Adaptation

**Benefit**

Profit and loss allow economic agents to evaluate experiments without
requiring a central authority to determine every successful or
unsuccessful use of resources.

This creates a powerful decentralized learning mechanism.

**Fragility**

Profit measures the local realized result more effectively than the
effect of that result on the wider system state.

It is therefore possible for:

Profit(i) > 0

while:

Δ Redistribution Capability(system) < 0

A profitable operation may simultaneously:

- reduce redundancy;
- transfer fragility;
- concentrate dependency;
- consume future optionality;
- narrow admissible trajectories.

**Boundary**

The structural boundary is reached when positive profit ceases to be a
reliable signal of systemically beneficial redistribution.

This boundary does not require profit itself to become negative.

**Potential Replacement / Extension**

Profit should not necessarily be eliminated.

A possible transition is:

Profit
→ Profit + State Diagnostics
→ Profit within Admissible State Constraints

This preserves decentralized feedback while incorporating the wider
state and trajectory of the system.

**Status:** HYPOTHESIS / DEVELOPING

## 5. Ownership

**Function**  
Control, responsibility, incentive, and allocation rights.

**Benefit**  
Creates durable claims and supports long-term decision-making.

**Fragility**  
Ownership can become separated from participation, consequences, and
systemic risk.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

---

## 6. Credit

**Function**  
Transfers future economic capacity into the present.

**Benefit**  
Accelerates investment and development.

**Fragility**  
Future states become constrained by obligations created in earlier
states.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

---

## 7. Labour

**Function**  
Human participation in production and primary distribution of income.

**Benefit**  
Historically links contribution, income, and resource access.

**Fragility**  
Automation may weaken the link between human labour and production
without removing the link between labour and income.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

---

## 8. Competition

**Function**  
Discovery, selection, adaptation, and efficiency pressure.

**Benefit**  
Encourages experimentation and removes persistently inefficient
structures.

**Fragility**  
Efficiency pressure may remove redundancy required for resilience.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

---

## 9. Bankruptcy

**Function**  
Local termination of failed economic structures.

**Benefit**  
Releases resources and prevents indefinite accumulation of local
failure.

**Fragility**  
Systemically important entities may become too interconnected to fail
locally.

**Boundary**  
When local failure threatens system-level continuity.

---

## 10. State Stabilization

**Function**  
Systemic buffering, redistribution, regulation, and crisis response.

**Benefit**  
Allows local failures and social shocks to be absorbed.

**Fragility**  
Fragility can be transferred from private/local systems to the
sovereign/macroeconomic level.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

---

## 11. Growth

**Function**  
Expansion of production, opportunity, and available economic space.

**Benefit**  
Historically allows conflicts over distribution to be softened through
expansion.

**Fragility**  
Continuous expansion may encounter physical, ecological, demographic,
or systemic boundaries.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.

---

## 12. Financial Markets

**Function**  
Capital allocation, liquidity provision, price discovery, and risk
transfer.

**Benefit**  
Rapid redistribution of financial resources.

**Fragility**  
Financial liquidity may become partially detached from the
Redistribution Capability of underlying real systems.

**Boundary**  
To be investigated.
