# WLRT Transition Economy

## Research Branch

This directory contains an exploratory research branch based on
Wave Liquidity Redistribution Theory (WLRT).

The purpose of this branch is to investigate the structural limits
of the contemporary capital-centered economic system and to examine
whether WLRT can provide a diagnostic and transitional framework for
the emergence of a different economic architecture.

The research does not begin from the assumption that capitalism must
be abolished, nor from a predefined post-capitalist ideology.

Instead, the investigation proceeds functionally.

For each major mechanism of the contemporary economic system, the
research asks:

1. What function does the mechanism perform?
2. What benefit does this function provide?
3. What fragility does the mechanism create or transfer?
4. Where is the structural boundary beyond which its benefit no
   longer compensates for its fragility?
5. Can the function be preserved through another mechanism?

The working analytical sequence is:

Function
→ Benefit
→ Fragility
→ Boundary
→ Replacement

The term "Replacement" does not necessarily imply elimination.
A mechanism may remain present while losing its dominant systemic role.

## Central Research Question

Can Wave Liquidity Redistribution Theory (WLRT) provide a roadmap
for a gradual transition from a capital-centered economy toward an
economic architecture in which the preservation and development of
Redistribution Capability becomes a major criterion of systemic
performance?

## Current Working Hypothesis

A possible transition may not require the destruction of markets,
money, private property, entrepreneurship, or profit.

Instead, these mechanisms may gradually become components of a broader
architecture in which price and capital are no longer sufficient
descriptions of system state and economic performance.

A preliminary transition direction is:

Capital-Centric Economy
→ Hybrid Transition Architecture
→ Redistribution-Capability Economy

This remains a research hypothesis, not an established conclusion
of WLRT.

## Relationship to Existing WLRT Work

This branch builds on concepts developed within WLRT, including:

- liquidity redistribution;
- Liquidity Fragility Index (LFI);
- Compensation and Fragility;
- phase time;
- adaptive state space;
- Redistribution Capability;
- system trajectories and admissible transitions.

It is also conceptually connected to the book *Beyond the Price*,
which develops the broader proposition that price alone does not
provide a sufficient description of the state, trajectory, or
resilience of a complex system.

## Research Status

Exploratory / Developing.

Nothing in this directory should automatically be treated as part of
the canonical core of WLRT.

Individual propositions should be explicitly classified as:

- OBSERVATION
- HYPOTHESIS
- DERIVED
- CANON

Only propositions deliberately promoted to CANON should be treated as
established elements of this research architecture.
